How we actually do it

The Basement Campaign

Moisture first, comfort second, style third. Skip step one and steps two and three grow mildew.

1

The moisture interrogation

Before one stud goes up, we investigate: efflorescence on the walls, past flooding, grading outside, gutters, sump pump health. St. Louis clay soil moves and Midwest storms don't negotiate. If there's a water problem, we fix the water problem first — finishing a wet basement is just gift-wrapping a swamp.

A $200 gutter fix now beats a $20,000 do-over later. The degrees are for math like that.
2

Layout & the boring-but-critical permits

Bedrooms need egress windows (code says a human must be able to climb out — we agree with code on this one). Furnace and water heater need service clearance, ceilings need height, and permits get pulled where required. We handle the paperwork. College made us good at forms; we hate it, but we're good at it.

3

Framing off the concrete

Walls framed with a gap off the foundation and pressure-treated bottom plates on the slab — wood that touches concrete without treatment is wood that's planning its own funeral. Everything plumb, straight, and laid out so drywall lands on center like it was born there.

4

Insulation & systems

Rigid foam against foundation walls (fiberglass alone against concrete is a moisture sponge — hard pass), then framed walls, wiring for outlets, lighting, and the 47 HDMI runs your theater deserves. Ductwork extended so the space actually heats and cools instead of being “the cold room with a couch.”

5

Drywall, ceiling & floors

Moisture-resistant board where it counts, drop ceiling or drywall depending on how much access you want to pipes above. Flooring is LVP over a subfloor system nine times out of ten — warm underfoot, and if the sump ever loses a fight, it survives.

6

The bar. Obviously.

Wet bar plumbing, cabinet run, mini-fridge alcove, LED strips set to whatever color the playoffs require. Final punch list, final clean, first movie night. We recommend something with explosions to test the subwoofer honestly.

Straight answer on ceilings: St. Louis basements run low. We'll tell you in the free walkthrough — to the inch — what's achievable, what needs soffit gymnastics, and what physics won't allow. No guy-code optimism, just tape-measure facts.

Everything That's Involved

Everything between bare concrete and “why do we even use the upstairs anymore.”

  • Moisture control — grading advice, sump checks, sealing, drainage — the non-negotiable first move
  • Egress windows — code-required escape windows for bedrooms, cut and flashed right
  • Framing — treated plates, straight walls, soffits that hide ducts with dignity
  • Insulation — rigid foam at foundation, sound insulation in ceilings if you have teenagers
  • Electrical — lighting layouts, outlets to code, theater and network wiring
  • HVAC extension — supply and return runs so the space is actually livable year-round
  • Plumbing — wet bars, bathroom rough-ins, ejector pits when gravity says no
  • Drywall & ceilings — hung, taped, finished — or drop ceiling for access, your call
  • Flooring — LVP, carpet tiles, subfloor systems that shrug off moisture
  • Bar & built-ins — cabinet runs, shelving, the shrine wall for your fantasy trophies
  • Paint & trim — full finish, doors hung and latched like upstairs quality
  • Final clean — movie-night ready, remote batteries not included
