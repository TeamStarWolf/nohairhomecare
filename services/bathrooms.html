Bathrooms: small room, zero margin for error.

A bathroom is 40 square feet where water is actively trying to destroy your house 24/7. This is the room where “good enough” guys get exposed. We are not those guys.

How we actually do it

The Bathroom Battle Plan

Waterproofing is a religion here. Everything else is decoration on top of it.

1

Scope & moisture check

We check the subfloor around the toilet and tub first — that's where the previous guy's sins live. Soft spots, old leaks, mystery caulk archaeology: we find it in the walkthrough so the quote covers reality, not fantasy.

2

Demo to the studs (when it's a gut)

Tile, backer, tub, vanity — out. We're looking at the framing and subfloor with our own eyes before anything new goes in. If there's rot, it gets cut out and sistered, not covered up like a bad haircut.

Carpet in a bathroom is not demo. It's an exorcism. We've done several.
3

Rough-in & framing

Tub-to-shower conversions, moving drains, adding niches (yes, you want a niche — where else does the shampoo live?), blocking for grab bars whether you need them now or in twenty years. Fan vented to the outside, not the attic — venting a fan into the attic is how you grow a mold farm.

4

Waterproofing: the sacred layer

Cement board or foam board, seams taped and sealed, then a full waterproof membrane on every wet wall and the pan. Shower floors slope 1/4 inch per foot to the drain — measured, not eyeballed. We flood-test pans before tile. If it can't hold water for 24 hours as a bathtub, it doesn't get to be a shower.

This layer is invisible the day we finish and it's the entire reason your ceiling below stays a ceiling.
5

Tile like we're being judged

Layout planned so cuts are even at both ends, leveled lines, spacers, and zero lippage — run your hand across our walls, it's glass. Grout sealed, movement joints caulked (corners get caulk, not grout — grout in corners cracks, and cracked corners are for other crews).

6

Fixtures, glass & final

Vanity set level, faucets and shower trim installed, toilet set with a new wax ring and bolted like it's going to outlive us all. Glass measured after tile. Then the punch-list walkthrough: bring a flashlight, try to win. Nobody has.

The one hair joke you get: the only hair removal happening at No Hair Home Care is us pulling the previous owner's hair clog out of your drain. It's disgusting. We do it anyway. That's love.

Everything That's Involved

The full anatomy of a bathroom job — every layer, including the ones you'll never see.

  • Demo & rot repair — tear-out to studs, subfloor replacement, joist sistering where needed
  • Plumbing rough-in — drain moves, valve replacement, tub-to-shower conversions
  • Ventilation — properly sized exhaust fans, ducted outside, on a timer if you're fancy
  • Waterproofing — membrane systems, flood-tested pans, sloped floors — the sacred layer
  • Tile — floors, walls, niches, curbs — leveled, planned, zero lippage
  • Heated floors — electric mat systems under tile, because Missouri winters are personal
  • Vanities & tops — stock, semi-custom, or built-in — set level, scribed tight
  • Toilets & fixtures — set right, sealed right, no rocking, no whistling fill valve
  • Glass & doors — shower glass coordination, pocket doors where swing space is tight
  • Lighting & electrical — GFCI everything, vanity lighting that doesn't make you look haunted
  • Paint & trim — moisture-resistant finishes, crisp lines, caulk like pinstripes
  • Accessibility — grab-bar blocking, curbless entries, comfort-height everything on request
